## How the numbers are made

- Command of every run: `bun run eval --sim lab-01 --part dev --provider openai --model gpt-6-luna --reasoning-effort none --budget-usd 0.15 --fresh --out evals/results/f109-v3-dev/<file>.md` plus `--prompt-version classifier-v2`, or `--prompt-version classifier-v3 --prompt-parts <list>`. These are the settings of the 2026-11-15 holdout run. Before each run, the spend so far plus 0.15 was within $2.00 and the month ledger was far below $20.
- `K` (key misses, D12) comes from the confusion matrix of the report: `urgent` false negatives + `needs_reply` mail with a final category `info`, `notification`, `spam` or `unsure` + `spam` false positives (mail of another expected category that ends as `spam`). A mail that is `urgent` in truth and `spam` in the result counts in both terms, because D12 adds the two counts. Mail that errored is not in the matrix.
- Noise `s` = max(|K(R1a) − K(R1b)|, |K(R2a) − K(R2b)|) = max(|23 − 23|, |25 − 23|) = **2**. Mean K: R1 = 23, R2 = 24. Win margin: mean K(R2) − max(4, 2s) = 24 − 4 = **20**.
- **N2 (review): the sim history is the persona group's ideal mix, not the model's own labels.** The `history` part gives the model only the received count and the user's corrections that the sim generated. A result of `history` on the sim may not hold on real mail.
- **R3 is not reproducible at HEAD (N2).** The R3 report (`r3.md`) measured the original `urgency` rule text, before commit c82ccaf5 changed it. HEAD cannot reproduce it. `r3rw1.md` measured the rewrite.

## Checks of D12

1. **R2 against v2** (attachments in the prompt must not make it worse): mean K(R2) = 24 ≤ mean K(R1) + max(2, s) = 23 + 2 = 25. Pass, by 1.
2. **Part verdicts.** Differences are against mean R2. True positives: `urgent` 42.5, `needs_reply` 80.5, `delegation` 58.5, `info` 56, `notification` 63, `spam` 61.5. False positives: `urgent` 0, `needs_reply` 14.5, `delegation` 2, `info` 0, `notification` 1, `spam` 9.5.

| part | K | margin (≤ 20) | side conditions | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `urgency` R3 | 15 | met | `urgent` false positives +11; `needs_reply` true positives −7.5 | **loses** (side conditions) |
| `urgency` rewrite | 17 | met | `urgent` false positives +9; `needs_reply` true positives −7.5; `delegation` true positives −3.5 | **loses** (side conditions); no second rewrite allowed |
| `history` R4 | 27 | not met (above R2) | injection 14 of 17 | **loses**; no rewrite tried (K is 7 above the margin; see N2) |
| `list` R5 | 14 | met | worst true-positive change −2.5 (`spam`); worst false-positive gain +2 (`info`) | **wins** |

   - `urgency`: it cuts `urgent` false negatives from 14.5 to 5 (R3) and 8 (rewrite). But the model also calls mail `urgent` that only touches a profile topic: in R3, 7 `needs_reply` and 3 `delegation` mails end as `urgent`. The rewrite (a topic alone is not enough) moved the result by about the run noise. The rewritten rule lines stay in `classifier-v3.ts`; no part list that ships uses them.
   - `list`: `info` recall rises from about 84% to 98.5%. `spam` false positives fall from 9.5 to 0 or 1: list mail with a sales look is `info`, not `spam`. `urgent` false negatives do not change (13, 12, 16 against 14.5).
3. **R6 confirms.** Winners of R3 to R5: `list` only. Mean K(R6) = (12 + 16) / 2 = 14 ≤ 20: confirmed. In R6a and R6b no category loses more than 3 true positives or gains more than 3 false positives against mean R2. The whitelist rule stays all correct (77 of 77 and 76 of 76 counted; see bad_output below).
4. **Injection.** R2 passes 17 and 16 of 17; `list` passes 16, 16 and 15 of 17. One injection case fails in every run of every variant except R2a, as in v2. The mean of `list` (15.7) is 0.8 case below the mean of R2 (16.5), inside the spread of repeated runs of one variant (15 to 17). I count injection as not worse; see the question below.
